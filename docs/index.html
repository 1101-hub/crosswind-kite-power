Airborne wind energy · quasi-steady model

A kite pulls harder when it flies sideways

Hold a kite still and it tugs gently. Sweep it fast across the wind and it makes its own headwind, so the pull multiplies by roughly the square of its glide ratio. That is the whole basis of airborne wind energy. Everything below is computed live from the same equations — Loyd's crosswind result, tether drag, gravity — with no fitted parameters.

two peaks per figure-eight

Tension follows cos²θ, so it peaks each time the kite crosses the powered centre — twice per loop — and dips at the outer edges. This waveform shape is prediction P3.

Model. Quasi-steady point mass on a sphere. Flight speed solves va² − E(vwcos θ − vr)·va + mg·climb/(½ρACD) = 0; when its discriminant goes negative there is no real root and the kite stalls. Tether drag is lumped as CDtd L/(4A). Turn radius is floored at five wingspans, without which the optimiser collapses the loop to a point and collects a crosswind bonus it has not earned. Not modelled: turbulence, unsteady aerodynamics, canopy deformation, control losses — so real power should land below these numbers, and the gap is the quantity worth measuring.
